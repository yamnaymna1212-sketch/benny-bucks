## What the winning script does (and the penguin scripts did not)
1. **Stake in the first 6 s = the VIEWER's money.** "Benny fills 10 gallons. The pump stops at $43.20. Where did my $43.20 go?" The penguin script spent 10 s on "Doug drives snowmobile to work every week" — no stake.
2. **Mystery, not lecture.** "Follow the money" with 2 open loops ("where does the other $21 go?", "then why is Iran so cheap?"), each answered later. "Reason 1 / 2 / 3" lets the viewer predict the end and leave after reason 1. Never use a numbered list.
3. **Every line = something you can SEE** (fills, receipt, pump stops, a number on screen). Abstract lines ("money melt", "bear pay") have no shot.
4. **Receipt-style numbers that add up** ($43.20 -> ~$21 oil -> $1.84 federal tax -> ~$3.30 state). The viewer can verify them, so it feels true.
5. **Natural adult English.** No caveman. Audience = US 25-34. Max ~12 words per sentence, one idea per sentence, a punch word at the end.
6. **Ending = one clean insight that closes the hook** ("it isn't about cheap oil, it's about who pays") + ONE binary comment question. The penguin ending was garbled ("double Team Iran gas or Team America paycheck").
7. **Facts must be right.** The penguin script said "Iran spends $50B to keep energy cheap in America" (wrong country, unverified). Every number needs a source in the episode's script.md before recording.

## 10-beat structure (61-65 s, ~170-190 words, 1 line = 1 shot)
| # | Time | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0-5 | Hook: "Why does X cost $A in America but $B in <country>? Y times cheaper." (two big numbers on screen, ting at 0 s) |
| 2 | 5-12 | Stake: Doug (or "you") pays. Show the receipt / pump / bill with the exact number |
| 3 | 12-15 | Open loop 1: "Wait. Where did my money go?" |
| 4 | 15-30 | Follow the money: 3 receipt numbers, one shot each |
| 5 | 30-33 | Open loop 2: "But <country> has the same <costs>. So why is it so cheap?" |
| 6 | 33-48 | The real reason (villain animal appears: Boss Bear / Larry / Tax Gull), with ONE concrete number |
| 7 | 48-54 | The catch / twist (a limit, a quota, a hidden cost) — second shock number |
| 8 | 54-58 | Insight line that closes the hook: "<Doug> didn't pay for X. <Villain> did." |
| 9 | 58-63 | CTA: one binary question "Team A or Team B?" tied to the story |
| 10 | last words | Last sentence loops back to the first (so the replay starts clean) |

## Delivery (voice direction)
- A friend telling gossip, not a narrator. Energy rises through the video; a drop in volume right before the key number, then say it clean.
- No flat pauses; max_gap ~0.08 s (as in EP7 v2). One "Wait, what?" moment around 12 s.
- Hook line is the loudest and fastest. Never start with "This is Doug."

## Sample: Iran gas, rewritten (penguin version of the 65K script, ~190 words)
1. Why does one gallon of gas cost four dollars and forty-one cents in America... but eleven cents in Iran? That's forty times cheaper.
2. Doug fills his snowmobile. Ten gallons. The pump stops at forty-four dollars and ten cents.
3. He stares at the receipt. Wait, what? Where did my forty-four dollars go?
4. So Doug follows the money.
5. Oil is only about half. Around twenty-two dollars.
6. Next number. Eighteen point four cents a gallon. That's the federal gas tax. One dollar eighty-four on Doug's ten gallons.
7. States add about three dollars more. So five dollars of this receipt is just tax.
8. But Doug is still stuck. Iran pays for oil too. So why is Iran's gas almost free?
9. Boss Bear. The government sells gas below cost, and Boss Bear pays the difference. Every gallon.
10. That's called a subsidy. (verify: Iran's yearly energy subsidy figure before saying a number)
11. But here's the catch. The first sixty liters a month are cheap. The next fifty cost double. After that, almost seven times more.
12. And Iran's money lost over ninety percent of its value. So those rials are pennies in dollars. (verify)
13. Doug didn't overpay for gas. Iran's Boss Bear underpaid for it, and then ran a limit.
14. Cheap gas with a monthly limit... or four-forty-one with no limit. Team Iran or Team America? Tell Doug.
