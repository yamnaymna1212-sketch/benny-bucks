Why Doug spend ten dollars with cash... but with card, twenty?
Same Doug. Same store. Something fishy.
This is Doug. Doug go to shop with one fish. One fish is ten dollars.
Doug hold fish. Fish cold. Fish wet. Doug feel every fish.
Doug buy one fish burger. Ten dollars. Done.
Next day, Doug bring card.
Card not cold. Card not wet. Card just go beep.
Beep. Burger. Beep. Fries. Beep. Big soda.
Twenty dollars. Doug did not feel anything.
Cash hurt when you pay. Card not hurt.
This is called pain of paying.
Doug.
In real life, one MIT study: people pay up to two times more with card.
And every beep, everybody take a cut.
Bank take a cut. Card company take a cut.
Store hide it in the price. Two percent. Every swipe.
So cash Doug pay for card Doug too.
Rich card get two percent back. Cash get nothing.
But here is the trick.
If Doug not pay card bill, card company charge twenty-one percent a year.
Small beep. Big bill. Every month.
After one year, twenty-dollar burger cost twenty-four.
Doug look at bill. Doug look at card. Card look happy.
Doug go back to fish. Doug feel every fish. Doug buy one burger.
Doug happy. Doug is smart penguin. Sometimes.
