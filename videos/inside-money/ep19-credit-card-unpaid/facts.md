# EP19 facts — "What if you stop paying your credit card?" (checked 2026-11-11)
Every number in the voice or on screen needs a line here. Re-check before posting.

- Late fee ~ $32 first / up to ~$43 repeat (CARD Act penalty-fee safe harbor, 12 CFR 1026.52(b), inflation-adjusted). The CFPB $8 cap was VACATED by N.D. Tex. on 2025-04-15 and never took effect (Holland & Knight https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/04/cfpb-credit-card-late-fees-rule-vacated-by-texas-district-court ; Goodwin https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2025/05/cfpb-agrees-to-eliminate-$8-cap-on-credit-card-late-fees). Say "about forty dollars" or "up to forty dollars" in the script, not an exact figure. TODO: confirm the 2026 adjusted amount in the Federal Register.
- Reported to credit bureaus once a payment is 30+ days late (Experian / Chase education pages: https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/credit-score/charge-offs).
- Penalty APR: often up to ~29.99 % after a missed payment (Experian https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-a-penalty-apr/). Say "close to thirty percent".
- Charge-off typically around 180 days past due; the debt is still owed and can go to a collector or to court (Capital One https://capitalone.com/credit-cards/blog/credit-card-charge-off ; Chase link above).
- Negative info generally stays on a credit report about 7 years (CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-long-does-negative-information-remain-on-my-credit-report-en-323/).
- Wage garnishment for credit card debt generally needs a court judgment first; some states (e.g. TX, PA) generally bar wage garnishment for consumer debt -> script says "can" (CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-a-debt-collector-garnish-my-bank-account-or-my-wages-en-1439/ ; ignoring a suit -> default judgment: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-happens-if-a-creditor-sues-me-and-i-dont-respond-or-if-i-ignore-a-court-summons-en-1439/).
- No jail just for unpaid credit card debt; a judge may issue a warrant only if you ignore a court order to appear (CFPB https://consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/could-i-be-arrested-if-i-dont-pay-back-my-payday-loan-en-1631). Collectors may not threaten arrest (FDCPA). Script: "You won't go to jail just for the debt."
- Card issuers offer hardship / lower-payment plans; call early, you don't need to be behind (CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/need-help-your-credit-card-debt-start-your-credit-card-company/ ; https://consumerfinance.gov/language/cfpb-in-english/act-fast-if-you-cant-pay-your-credit-cards). Script: "can get you a payment plan" (not a promise).
