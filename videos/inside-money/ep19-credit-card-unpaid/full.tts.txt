What if you just stopped paying your credit card, forever? Mike owes one thousand dollars. He throws the bill in the trash. Day one. Nothing happens. Mike thinks he's a genius. He orders a pizza to celebrate. Day two. A late fee shows up. About thirty dollars, just for being late. Day thirty. The bank tells the credit bureaus. His credit score falls off a cliff. Ouch. Now his interest rate can jump close to thirty percent. His thousand dollars starts growing, like a pet monster. Every month, the interest feeds it, and the monster gets bigger. Day sixty. Red letters. Calls. His phone won't stop buzzing. Mike hides under a blanket, like it's a horror movie. Day one hundred eighty. The bank gives up and writes it off. Mike celebrates. Big mistake. The debt didn't disappear. It got sold to a collector, and the collector wants the money back. If he gets sued and ignores it, a judge can order part of his paycheck taken. And that late mark stays on his credit report for about seven years. Landlords check it. Car lenders check it. Apartments and car loans get way harder. The good news? You won't go to jail just for the debt. And calling the bank early can get you a payment plan, with lower payments, sometimes lower interest.
