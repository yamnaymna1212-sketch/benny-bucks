What if a criminal washed his dirty money, then deposited it at the bank? Would they... catch him? He walks in with fifty thousand dollars in cash. But over ten thousand dollars in cash, the bank files a report. It's routine. It happens to everyone. And that report shows his name, his address, and the cash. So next time, he gets smarter. He walks into Mike's burger shop. Mike owns this place. About fifty customers a day. The masked man makes an offer: put my cash in your register, and call it sales. Mike says no. He knows what fifty customers can really pay. But the bag is already on his counter. Here's what criminals don't know. Banks watch patterns, not just big numbers. A small burger shop that suddenly deposits three times more cash? That's a red flag. The bank already knows what a normal burger shop deposits, every single week. And splitting cash into small deposits to dodge the report? That's a crime by itself, even if the money was clean. Behind the glass, the bank sends a quiet report. Nobody is told. Not the criminal. Not Mike. The report doesn't prove anything. It just starts the questions. Three weeks later, a black car stops outside the burger shop. Two agents step out. I R S.
