# EP12 facts (checked 2026-11-04 via web search; re-verify on fincen.gov / justice.gov before publishing)
- Banks must file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) for cash over $10,000 in one business day; routine, applies to everyone, not an accusation. (FinCEN)
- Structuring (splitting cash to avoid the CTR) is a federal crime by itself, 31 U.S.C. 5324; DOJ jury instruction CRM 2183. Penalty sources vary (up to 5 years) -> do not state a number in the video.
- Banks file Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR) on structuring/odd activity without telling the customer. (FinCEN guidance)
- Real pizza-shop cases (DOJ press releases): Manco & Manco, Ocean City NJ (skimmed cash, deposits under $10,000, ~$981,000 concealed, guilty pleas); Mystic Pizza CT (structuring, sentenced); a PA-area owner whose cash deposits were 1.3% of deposits vs 40%+ cash sales. Allegations unproven until pleaded/convicted. These are TAX/STRUCTURING cases, not proof of the story.
- "50 customers a day" and "$100,000" are illustrative story numbers; on-screen label "Fictional story. Education only."
- CTR content: the form identifies the person conducting the transaction (name, address, ID) and the amount (FinCEN Form 112). Script: "that report shows his name, his address, and the cash."
- Structuring is a crime even if the money is legal (31 U.S.C. 5324: the offence is evading the report, not the source). Script: "even if the money was clean."
- A SAR is filed without telling the customer and is not proof of a crime (FinCEN guidance). Script: "Nobody is told... The report doesn't prove anything. It just starts the questions."
