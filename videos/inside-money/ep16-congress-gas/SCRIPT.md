Trump just lost Congress. So will gas get cheaper? Three things decide it. Meet Mike. He pays about four dollars a gallon. A year ago, he paid about three dollars and eight cents. That is about fourteen dollars more for every tank. Thing one, the tax. Every gallon carries an eighteen point four cent federal gas tax. It has not changed since nineteen ninety three. Only Congress can pause it, and this new Congress could try. But do the math. Mike fills a fifteen gallon tank. A pause saves him about two dollars and seventy six cents. That is less than five percent. Thing two, oil. About half of every gallon is crude oil, and crude is priced on the world market, not in Washington. Refining is about a quarter of the price. Taxes and delivery are the rest. About one fifth of the world's oil sails through one narrow strait, Hormuz. That is about twenty million barrels a day. This year, that shipping was disrupted. No vote in Congress can open it. Thing three, the president. A gas tax pause is a law. Back in May, Trump himself floated a gas tax pause. Congress passes it, then the president signs it, or vetoes it. To override a veto, Congress needs two thirds. So Congress controls one small slice. The world controls the biggest slice. And the president still holds the pen. Will gas be cheaper next year? Nobody can promise that. What do you think, up or down? Comment one word.
